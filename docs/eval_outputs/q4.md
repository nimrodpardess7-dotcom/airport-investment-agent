# Q4: What is the unmet flight demand in SFO airport and why?

**SFO is short of runway capacity, not demand.** The estimate is about **14,695 departures (2.06M boardings)** of unmet demand. A runway limit since March 2026 sets an upper bound of up to **238 arrivals a day**. The label is **Airfield**: a bigger terminal won't add landings while the runway limit binds. The biggest risk is timing. The new runway limit falls inside only about 1 month of the 12-month data window, so the data barely shows it yet.

**How much (two separate views; don't add them together)**
- **Recovery-gap estimate:** SFO passengers are at **-4.4%** vs 2019, while similar airports have a median of **+2.9%**. That gap works out to **2.06M boardings, or 14,695 departures** [data: T-100, 2025-05 to 2026-04] [assumption: A-24].
- **Capacity-loss upper bound:** clear-weather arrivals fell from **54 to 40 per hour, 14 fewer (25.9%)** [domain: SFO-02]. If every lost slot were filled for **17 hours a day**, that is **238 arrivals a day**, **33,324 passengers a day**, or **12.16M passengers a year** [assumption: A-10]. The real loss is smaller because of low-visibility and off-peak hours.

**Why: runways and airspace**
- **Airfield test passed on both conditions.** Runway and air-traffic-control delays make up **46.5%** of delays, ranked **p100** (the bar is p75). The runway layout is also documented as a limit: the parallel runways are 750 ft apart, too close for independent approaches [domain: SFO-01] (news report, unconfirmed). There are no near misses.
- **The limit got tighter.** Since March 31, 2026 the FAA no longer allows side-by-side landings, even in clear weather. Capacity fell to 36 per hour. Staggered approaches raised it to 40 from Aug 12, 2026, with 42 planned [domain: SFO-02] (news report, unconfirmed).
- **SFO has been a schedule-facilitated (IATA Level 2) airport since 2012** because of runway capacity and delays. Airline schedules are reviewed voluntarily, so there is no legal cap [domain: SFO-03].

**Competing explanation: weaker demand or weaker routes**
- **Against it:** planes are **82.6%** full (**p97**), which doesn't fit weak demand.
- **For it:** departures are **-10.7%** (**p30**) and passengers **-4.4%** (**p27**) vs 2019. A weaker route network would also produce this gap [assumption: A-24]. Planes are bigger (seats per departure **+6.4%**, p33), but the tool doesn't count this as the pattern of airlines using bigger planes to work around a capacity limit [assumption: A-09].

**Caveats**
- Unmet demand can't be observed directly. These are indirect signs plus two estimates. Overall confidence is **medium**.
